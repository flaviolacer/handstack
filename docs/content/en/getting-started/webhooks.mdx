# Deliver signed webhooks safely

`@handstack/webhooks` signs the canonical JSON body with HMAC-SHA256. Receivers must verify the
`sha256=` signature with a constant-time comparison before processing an event.

The dispatcher retries transient transport failures with bounded exponential backoff. A poison
delivery is retained in a dead-letter collection with its organization, event, attempt count and
redacted error for operator recovery.

Use a unique delivery id for idempotency and rotate endpoint secrets through the secret provider.

## Prerequisites

- A tenant webhook endpoint and a secret of at least 16 characters.

## Steps

1. Register the endpoint and keep its secret outside source control.
2. Dispatch events with a unique delivery id.
3. Verify `sha256=` before accepting the body and deduplicate the id.

The tenant-scoped API exposes `POST /api/v1/organizations/:organizationId/webhooks/configuration`,
`POST .../webhooks/deliveries`, `GET .../webhooks/deliveries`, `GET .../webhooks/dead-letters`, and
`POST .../webhooks/dead-letters/:deliveryId/replay`. All routes require the
`webhooks.manage` permission and exact organization scope.

Use `GET .../webhooks/deliveries?status=DEAD_LETTERED` to inspect a filtered delivery history.
The response is tenant-scoped and never includes configured webhook secrets.

## Verify

Force one transient failure and confirm the second attempt succeeds; force a poison endpoint and
confirm the delivery appears in dead-letter state.

## Troubleshooting

- A signature mismatch means the exact raw body or secret changed before verification.
- Dead-letter records require operator replay after the endpoint is repaired.
- An invalid `status` query is rejected; use `PENDING`, `DELIVERED`, `RETRYING`, or `DEAD_LETTERED`.
